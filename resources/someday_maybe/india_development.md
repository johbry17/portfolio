# India: Development Without Convergence?

*Economic growth, human development, and the uneven geography of structural transformation*

**Status:** Someday / Maybe  
**Project type:** Field Note / Development Economics / Regional Analysis  
**Geographic scope:** Indian states  
**Proposed study period:** Approximately 1991–2021, subject to data availability and comparability

## Research question

Has India's economic development produced convergence in living standards, or are its states experiencing increasingly divergent development trajectories?

India's national economic growth story can obscure substantial differences in how individual states develop. States differ considerably in economic performance, industrial structure, demographic transitions, educational attainment, and human development outcomes.

This study would examine the uneven geography of India's development, investigating whether economic growth translates into broadly shared improvements in living standards and whether different states are following fundamentally different development pathways.

## Three analytical themes

### 1. Growth versus convergence

**Are poorer states catching up with wealthier states?**

Examine the distribution and evolution of economic performance across Indian states.

Potential indicators:
- Per-capita Net State Domestic Product (NSDP).
- Real per-capita income growth.
- State contributions to national GDP.
- Relative economic performance and changing state rankings.
- Measures of interstate income inequality, such as the coefficient of variation.

Investigate whether poorer states are experiencing faster growth than wealthier states, and whether regional economic disparities are narrowing or widening.

### 2. Income versus human development

**Does greater economic prosperity translate into better development outcomes?**

Examine the relationship between state income and improvements in human well-being.

Potential indicators:
- Life expectancy.
- Infant and maternal mortality.
- Total fertility rates.
- Literacy and educational attainment.
- Multidimensional poverty.
- Access to healthcare and basic services.

Identify states that outperform or underperform relative to their economic resources.

Explore whether some states achieve comparatively strong human development outcomes without equivalent levels of economic prosperity.

### 3. Economic transformation versus inclusion

**Who benefits from structural economic change?**

Examine how state economies have evolved and whether structural transformation has produced broadly distributed economic opportunities.

Potential indicators:
- Agriculture, manufacturing, and services as shares of state economic output.
- Sectoral employment distribution.
- Female labor-force participation.
- Urbanization.
- Industrial employment and productivity.
- Agricultural employment and economic contribution.

Investigate whether states are following different development pathways, and whether economic growth is accompanied by meaningful changes in employment structure and economic inclusion.

## Potential analytical approach

A primarily descriptive and comparative analysis, emphasizing longitudinal patterns and differences between states.

Potential methods and visualizations:
- State-level choropleth maps.
- Small-multiple time-series comparisons.
- Per-capita income convergence/divergence analysis.
- Scatterplots comparing economic prosperity and human development.
- State ranking and trajectory visualizations.
- Sectoral composition and structural transformation charts.
- Regional comparisons (northern, southern, eastern, and western India).

Possible comparative case studies include Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Bihar, and Uttar Pradesh, selected to illustrate contrasting economic and human development trajectories.

The objective is not to produce a definitive ranking of states, but to identify meaningful patterns, differences, and development trade-offs.

## Data sources

### Primary sources

**1. Reserve Bank of India (RBI) — Handbook of Statistics on Indian States**

https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/AnnualPublications.aspx?head=Handbook+of+Statistics+on+Indian+States

Likely foundational source. Consolidates state-level economic, demographic, health, agricultural, industrial, infrastructure, and fiscal indicators.

Particularly useful for:
- State domestic product and per-capita income.
- Sectoral economic composition.
- Demographic and health indicators.
- Infrastructure and industrial development.
- Employment and other socioeconomic measures.

**2. National Family Health Survey (NFHS)**

https://rchiips.org/nfhs/

Repeated nationally representative household surveys, providing state- and district-level information on health, fertility, education, gender, nutrition, and living conditions.

Survey rounds:
- NFHS-1: 1992–93
- NFHS-2: 1998–99
- NFHS-3: 2005–06
- NFHS-4: 2015–16
- NFHS-5: 2019–21

Particularly valuable for examining long-term human development and demographic change.

**3. Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI)**

https://www.mospi.gov.in/

National statistical publications, national accounts, employment surveys, and household consumption data.

Potentially useful for:
- Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS).
- National Sample Survey (NSS).
- National accounts and economic structure.
- Employment and labor-force participation.

**4. Census of India**

https://censusindia.gov.in/

Historical demographic, educational, occupational, and urbanization data.

Potentially useful for:
- Population structure and growth.
- Literacy.
- Urbanization.
- Occupational distribution.
- Migration.

Census data provide valuable historical benchmarks, although the decennial nature of the data limits temporal resolution.

## Originality and intellectual framing

The broad subjects—regional economic convergence, demographic transition, and structural transformation—are well-established areas of development economics. The project should not present these questions as novel discoveries.

Its potential contribution would be an integrated, accessible examination of three interconnected dimensions of development:

- Territorial convergence: Are states becoming more economically similar?
- Human development: Are improvements in economic prosperity translating into better lives?
- Inclusive transformation: Is structural economic change producing broadly distributed opportunities?

A particularly interesting possibility is that states may be converging on some development indicators while diverging on others.

The distinction between economic convergence, human development convergence, and inclusive economic transformation could provide the central analytical narrative.

## Feasibility and limitations

**Preliminary feasibility: High, with methodological qualifications.**

The RBI and NFHS provide unusually rich sources of state-level longitudinal data. The principal challenge is not finding data, but establishing comparability across time.

Issues requiring attention:
- Changes in state boundaries and administrative geography.
- Revisions to national and state accounting methodologies.
- Different base years for real economic output.
- Inconsistent historical coverage across indicators.
- Changes in survey methodology and definitions.
- Gaps between Census and household survey rounds.

A narrower, defensible study is preferable to assembling a large collection of indicators with incompatible historical coverage.

The proposed 1991–2021 period is an initial research window, not a commitment that every variable can be compared across all years.

## Potential central argument

India's development is not a single national trajectory. Its states are experiencing different combinations of economic growth, demographic transition, human development, and structural transformation.

Understanding these differences requires looking beyond aggregate national growth to examine how development unfolds across regions—and whether economic progress translates into broadly shared improvements in human well-being.

**Desired deliverable:** A compact, reproducible Quarto Field Note with a clear narrative, a small number of substantive visualizations, transparent data documentation, and a careful discussion of findings and limitations.
