# The Invisible River
**DC's Hidden Hydrology: Watersheds, Stormwater, and the Urban Landscape**

**Concept:** Investigate the hydrology beneath and around Washington, DC: how the natural watershed, urban development, impervious surfaces, buried streams, and stormwater infrastructure interact to shape flooding and water management.

**Core questions**
- How does DC's natural drainage geography compare with its modern urban landscape?
- Where have development and impervious surfaces most altered natural drainage?
- How do stormwater infrastructure and buried waterways redirect water?
- Which neighborhoods and watersheds face the greatest interior flooding risks?
- How does DC manage the relationship between urban development, water quality, and the Chesapeake Bay watershed?

**Potential analysis / visualizations**
- Watershed and subwatershed map of DC.
- Natural drainage patterns versus modern stormwater infrastructure.
- Impervious surface coverage by watershed or neighborhood.
- Tree canopy and pervious surface comparisons.
- Flood-risk geography: riverine, coastal, and interior flooding.
- Map of stormwater infrastructure, green infrastructure, and retention facilities.
- A visual exploration of historical streams and waterways, where historical mapping is available.

**Potential data sources**
- [USGS DC StreamStats](https://streamstats.usgs.gov/) — stormwater pipes, drainage basin delineation, watershed characteristics.
- [USGS StreamStats DC data releases](https://www.usgs.gov/data/data-support-washington-dc-streamstats-project-washington-dc-and-parts-delaware-maryland-new) — high-resolution elevation, flow direction, catchments, and stormwater-network-informed hydrology.
- [DC Open Data](https://opendata.dc.gov/) — watersheds, impervious surfaces, stormwater infrastructure, environmental layers.
- [USGS DC Watersheds](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/dc-watersheds) — hydrologic unit boundaries.
- [DOEE Flood Risk in the District](https://doee.dc.gov/service/flood-risk-district) — flooding classifications and risk information.
- [FEMA National Flood Hazard Layer](https://www.fema.gov/flood-maps/national-flood-hazard-layer) — mapped riverine and coastal flood hazards.
- [USGS National Hydrography Dataset](https://www.usgs.gov/national-hydrography) — streams, waterbodies, and hydrographic features.
- [EPA How's My Waterway](https://mywaterway.epa.gov/) — water quality and watershed information.

**Potential skills:** GIS, spatial analysis, hydrology, environmental data, raster analysis, R/Python, cartography, data storytelling.

**Scope:** Medium-sized Field Note. Could start as a visually compelling watershed and impervious-surface map, then expand into stormwater and flood-risk analysis.

**Potential takeaway:** The city is not just built around its rivers. It is built on top of, and continually reshaping, a much larger hydrological system.
