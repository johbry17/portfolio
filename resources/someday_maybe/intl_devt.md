# International Development Data — Mini-Project Ideas

Quick portfolio projects to bridge international development / international affairs experience with data analytics. Designed for approximately 1–2 focused sessions each, emphasizing public data, reproducible workflows, and concise analytical findings.

## 1. U.S. Foreign Assistance at a Glance

**Concept:** Analyze U.S. foreign assistance by recipient country, sector, implementing agency, and fiscal year. Build a concise dashboard or analytical report highlighting three meaningful findings.

**Potential research questions:**
- Which countries receive the most U.S. foreign assistance?
- How is funding distributed across development sectors?
- Which U.S. agencies administer or implement the assistance?
- How has the composition of foreign assistance changed over time?

**Data sources:**
- [ForeignAssistance.gov — API Documentation](https://foreignassistance.gov/api-docs) — Official U.S. foreign assistance data, including country, sector, agency, and financial information.
- [ForeignAssistance.gov](https://foreignassistance.gov/) — Data portal and downloadable datasets.

**Skills demonstrated:** API/data ingestion, ETL, data cleaning, aggregation, visualization, and international development policy interpretation.

**Deliverable:** Small Python/SQL analysis with 2–3 visualizations and a concise README or Quarto report.

## 2. Who Funds What? — U.S. Foreign Assistance Allocation

**Concept:** Examine the distribution of U.S. foreign assistance across sectors and implementing agencies. Focus on funding composition and changes over time rather than simply ranking recipients by total spending.

**Potential research questions:**
- How is U.S. foreign assistance distributed across humanitarian aid, health, economic development, governance, and other sectors?
- Which agencies administer assistance in different sectors?
- How have sectoral funding priorities shifted over time?
- Are there notable differences in funding composition across recipient countries?

**Data sources:**
- [ForeignAssistance.gov — API Documentation](https://foreignassistance.gov/api-docs)
- [ForeignAssistance.gov — Data Portal](https://foreignassistance.gov/)

**Skills demonstrated:** SQL, GROUP BY analysis, financial data interpretation, comparative analysis, and data visualization.

**Deliverable:** A compact analytical report or dashboard showing funding composition by sector, agency, and year.

## 3. SDG Progress Dashboard

**Concept:** Compare a selection of Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) indicators across countries. Examine progress over time while making missing data and measurement limitations visible.

**Potential research questions:**
- How are selected countries progressing toward specific SDG targets?
- Where are the largest differences between countries?
- Which indicators show improvement, stagnation, or regression?
- How much of the available indicator data is missing or outdated?

**Potential scope:** Select 3–5 indicators from 2–3 SDGs and compare a handful of countries, potentially including India and Costa Rica.

**Data sources:**
- [UN SDG Global Database](https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/dataportal) — Official global SDG indicator data.
- [UN SDG API](https://unstats.un.org/SDGAPI/swagger/) — Programmatic access to SDG indicator data.
- [World Bank Indicators API](https://datahelpdesk.worldbank.org/knowledgebase/articles/889392) — Complementary country-level development indicators and historical time series.
- [Our World in Data — SDG Tracker](https://ourworldindata.org/sdgs) — Accessible indicator visualizations and downloadable data.

**Skills demonstrated:** API/data integration, time-series analysis, indicator interpretation, cross-country comparisons, visualization, and international development literacy.

**Deliverable:** Interactive dashboard or Quarto HTML report with comparative charts, country profiles, and data completeness indicators.

---

## Portfolio Objective

**Primary purpose:** Establish a visible connection between international development domain expertise and technical data analytics skills.

These are deliberately small projects, not comprehensive research studies. Prioritize:
- Publicly accessible, reproducible data.
- Clear research questions.
- Competent data cleaning and analysis.
- A handful of meaningful visualizations.
- Transparent documentation of sources, definitions, and limitations.

**Time constraint:** Approximately 1–2 focused sessions per project. Avoid scope creep; publish a credible first version and move on to the next portfolio gap.
