# Who Owns Washington?
**The Geography of Land Ownership, Federal Power, and Public Space in Washington, DC**

**Concept:** Map and investigate the extraordinary geography of land ownership and jurisdiction in the nation's capital. Explore how federal, District, institutional, and private ownership shape the physical city and the relationship between its residents and government.

**Core questions**
- How much of DC's land is federally owned versus District-owned or privately held?
- Where are federal properties concentrated?
- How much land is devoted to parks, monuments, government buildings, and other public purposes?
- How do ownership, jurisdiction, management, and public access differ?
- How does the federal government's physical footprint shape the city's geography?
- Which neighborhoods have the greatest concentration of public versus private land?

**Potential analysis / visualizations**
- Citywide parcel map classified by ownership.
- Federal versus District versus private land by area.
- Ownership composition by ward or neighborhood.
- Map of federal reservations, national parks, monuments, and government facilities.
- Public land versus publicly accessible land (an important distinction).
- Spatial relationship between federal land, commercial development, and residential neighborhoods.
- A closer look at unusual jurisdictional boundaries and transfers of property between the federal government and DC.

**Potential data sources**
- [DC Open Data](https://opendata.dc.gov/) — primary source for property, land, and jurisdictional GIS layers.
- [Public Lands in DC](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/public-lands) — government public lands and ownership information.
- [Property Lots in DC](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/property-lots-in-dc) — parcel-level geography, including federal lands, reservations, and District properties.
- [District Government Land](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/district-government-land-owned-operated-and-or-managed) — District properties that are owned, operated, or managed by government agencies.
- [Federal Land (RPTA Ownership)](https://maps2.dcgis.dc.gov/dcgis/rest/services/DCGIS_DATA/Property_and_Land/MapServer/50) — federal ownership GIS layer.
- [DC Real Property Finder](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/dc-real-property-finder) — parcel boundaries, tax assessments, zoning, and related property information.
- [NPS Data and Maps](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/gisandmapping/index.htm) — national park boundaries, reservations, and federal park geography.
- [USASpending.gov](https://www.usaspending.gov/) — potential supplementary analysis of federal investment and contracting geographically.

**Potential skills:** GIS, spatial joins, geospatial classification, data cleaning, cartography, public policy, political economy, data storytelling.

**Scope:** Medium-sized Field Note. Begin with parcel ownership classification and citywide mapping. Expand into jurisdiction, public access, and the federal government's physical presence if the data supports it.

**Important methodological consideration:** Ownership, jurisdiction, operation, management, and public access are not interchangeable. A federal reservation, a District-managed property, and a publicly accessible park may have different legal and administrative characteristics. Establish a defensible classification system before calculating ownership shares.

**Potential takeaway:** Washington is not simply a city that happens to contain the federal government. The federal government's presence is embedded in the city's physical geography, land tenure, and public spaces.
